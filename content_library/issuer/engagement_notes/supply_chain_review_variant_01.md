## Purpose of Engagement

This engagement was initiated by the stewardship team, separately from routine concentration monitoring of the position, to assess {{ISSUER_NAME}}'s management of labour and human rights risk in its hardware and data centre supply chain and in its extended contingent workforce. The topic was prioritised following NGO reporting on working conditions at contract manufacturers and data-labelling vendors serving large technology platforms, and because the company's expanding AI infrastructure build-out increases its exposure to tier 2 and tier 3 component suppliers in Asia.

---

## Key Discussion Points

**Supplier Audit Coverage**: Management reported that supplier responsibility audits now cover {{SUPPLIER_COVERAGE}}% of direct hardware spend. Coverage of tier 2 suppliers, notably in semiconductor packaging, server assembly and mineral processing, remains materially lower. The company identified {{SUPPLIER_ISSUES}} suppliers with findings on working hours, recruitment fees or dormitory conditions that are now under corrective action plans.

**Forced Labour and Recruitment Fees**: We asked how the company enforces its zero recruitment-fee policy for migrant workers. Management confirmed that fee repayment is required where violations are found, but could not yet quantify the total remediated. We requested disclosure of repayment totals and the number of workers affected.

**Contingent and Outsourced Workforce**: The discussion covered content moderators, data-labelling and raters employed through third-party vendors, who form a substantial share of the workforce supporting AI model development. We raised wage floors, access to mental health support for content moderation roles, and freedom of association. Management pointed to its supplier code wage and benefits standards but acknowledged that monitoring of vendor compliance is inconsistent across regions.

**Conflict Minerals and Traceability**: The company described smelter and refiner mapping for tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold, and an expansion of traceability work to cobalt and other minerals used in data centre hardware. We encouraged alignment with the OECD Due Diligence Guidance across all minerals in scope.

**Grievance Mechanisms**: Worker grievance channels exist at audited sites but usage data is not published. We asked for disclosure of grievance volumes, resolution times and categories.

---

## Management Commitments and Next Steps

**Company Commitments**:
1. Extend independent audits to priority tier 2 hardware suppliers within the next 12 months
2. Publish recruitment-fee remediation totals in the next supplier responsibility report
3. Report on vendor compliance with wage and benefits standards for the contingent AI workforce
4. Disclose grievance mechanism usage statistics at audited sites

**SAM Follow-up Actions**:
1. Track tier 2 audit coverage in the next supplier responsibility report
2. Review recruitment-fee remediation disclosure against the commitment above
3. Co-sign a collaborative investor letter on contingent workforce standards in AI supply chains
4. Reassess at the next quarterly ESG review and escalate through proxy voting if disclosure does not improve

**Next Engagement**: Follow-up meeting in six months to review progress on tier 2 audit coverage and contingent workforce disclosure.

---

## SAM Assessment

{{ISSUER_NAME}} has mature policies and good tier 1 audit coverage, but disclosure on outcomes (remediation totals, grievance data, contingent workforce conditions) lags peers with hardware-heavy supply chains. Supply chain labour risk is rising as the company scales AI infrastructure, and we consider it a material social risk for the holding. Engagement status: **Active - monitoring commitments**. This engagement is independent of, and additional to, the concentration limit discussions logged for this position.
